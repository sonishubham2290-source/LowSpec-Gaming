How to Fix Low FPS and Stuttering in PC Games: 12 Practical Fixes

Struggling with lag spikes, freezing, and poor frame rates? Here are 12 practical, step-by-step solutions to restore smooth performance on low-spec systems.

Few things ruin a gaming session faster than sudden frame drops and constant stuttering. While higher-end systems can brute-force their way through unoptimized games, low-spec systems rely heavily on proper system configuration. Below is a complete list of 12 actionable steps to eliminate lag and maximize your frames per second (FPS).

1. Enable Game Mode in Windows

Windows 10 and 11 feature a built-in Game Mode that prioritizes CPU and GPU resources for running games while restricting background system processes.

How to enable: Go to Settings > Gaming > Game Mode and toggle it to On.

2. Update Your Graphics Drivers

Outdated GPU drivers are one of the most common causes of game instability and micro-stuttering. Both integrated and dedicated graphics processors regularly receive optimization updates for newer games.

  • NVIDIA: Update via GeForce Experience or direct installer.
  • AMD: Update using AMD Software: Adrenalin Edition.
  • Intel: Download the latest Drivers from Intel Driver & Support Assistant.

3. Adjust In-Game Graphic Settings Correctly

Lowering the right settings can dramatically improve your frame times without completely ruining visual fidelity.

Setting Performance Impact Recommended Action
Shadow Quality Very High Set to Low or Medium
Volumetric Fog/Clouds High Set to Low or Off
Anti-Aliasing (MSAA) High Use FXAA or TAA instead
Screen Resolution Very High Lower resolution or use scaling

4. Change Power Plan to High Performance

By default, laptops and desktop PCs often use a balanced power plan that limits CPU frequency to save power. Switching to high performance ensures your processor runs at maximum speed while gaming.

Navigate to Control Panel > Power Options and select High Performance or Ultimate Performance.

5. Disable Background Apps and Startup Programs

Applications like web browsers, Discord, recording software, and heavy cloud synchronization services consume RAM and CPU cycles. Press Ctrl + Shift + Esc to open Task Manager, check the Processes tab, and close memory-heavy applications before launching your game.

6. Clean Temporary Files and Cache

Accumulated cache files and system junk can slow down drive read speeds, causing stuttering when games stream assets during gameplay.

Press Win + R, type %temp%, press Enter, and clear the files inside the folder.

7. Enable Upscaling (FSR, DLSS, or NIS)

Spatial and temporal upscaling technologies allow your PC to render games at a lower internal resolution and scale the image back up with minimal quality loss.

  • AMD FSR: Works across almost all GPUs (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel).
  • NVIDIA DLSS: Supported on RTX cards for massive performance gains.
  • NVIDIA Image Scaling (NIS): Driver-level upscaling for older GTX graphics cards.

8. Cap Your Frame Rate

Uncapped frame rates cause your GPU to constantly fluctuate between high and low performance, creating visible stuttering. Locking your FPS to a stable target (such as 30, 45, or 60 FPS) via RivaTuner Statistics Server (RTSS) or in-game settings delivers much smoother frame delivery.

9. Check for Overheating and Thermal Throttling

When your CPU or GPU exceeds safe thermal limits (usually above 85°C–90°C), it automatically drops clock speeds to protect hardware, leading to sudden FPS drops. Use software like HWMonitor or MSI Afterburner to monitor temperatures and clean out dust from your cooling fans.

10. Optimize Windows Virtual Memory (Pagefile)

If your PC has 8GB of RAM or less, running out of memory forces Windows to swap data to the storage drive, leading to heavy micro-stuttering. Manually setting a larger virtual memory allocation on a fast SSD helps buffer low RAM capacity.

11. Disable Overlays

Multiple active software overlays running simultaneously can conflict with game render engines and trigger lag.

  • Disable Discord Overlay in Discord settings.
  • Disable Steam Overlay via Steam Properties.
  • Disable GeForce Experience In-Game Overlay if not actively recording.

12. Move Games to an SSD

Modern games stream textures and assets continuously. Installing games on a traditional mechanical Hard Disk Drive (HDD) often causes stuttering when entering new zones. Moving your game installation to a Solid State Drive (SSD) greatly speeds up asset loading times.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why does my game stutter even with high FPS?
A: Stuttering with high average FPS usually happens due to unstable frame timing, CPU bottlenecks, background software interference, or RAM running out of capacity.

Q: Will adding more RAM increase my FPS?
A: If your system currently runs out of RAM while playing, adding more memory (and running in dual-channel mode) will significantly reduce micro-stuttering and increase overall FPS stability.

Q: Is 30 FPS playable for low-spec PC gaming?
A: Yes, a locked, consistent 30 FPS with smooth frame pacing offers a much better experience than an unlocked frame rate swinging wildly between 25 and 50 FPS.

Conclusion

Fixing low FPS and game stuttering doesn't always require buying expensive new hardware. By disabling background clutter, optimizing settings like shadow quality and upscaling, keeping thermal levels in check, and maintaining driver updates, you can enjoy fluid gameplay even on budget PCs.
